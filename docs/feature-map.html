Divebomb feature map

A pitch plugin recreating seven DigiTech pedals: four treadle Whammys and three footswitch pitch pedals, in the Flip Fella dark family. Research and plan, 6 November 2026. Questions answered the same day. The build waits for mockup approval.

RecommendationOne treadle engine with a Model selector (Whammy 5, DT, MonoNeon, Bass), sharing one tracker, one treadle and one bass path. The models differ only in their settings list, extra panel and character. Everything pitch-related runs through one shifter per voice that adds every active shift together, so stacking a drop tune on a whammy never stacks latency.

All DSP will be original code written from the manuals and public descriptions. The plugin UI names no brands or pedals, and "Whammy" is DigiTech's trademark, so the panel uses plain words: the Model card reads FIVE, DUAL, NEON and BASS, the setting groups read BEND, HARMONY, OCTAVE and DETUNE, and the footswitch pedals read DROP, HAMMER and GLIDE. This document uses the real names so you can check it against the manuals.

One engine or separate modes

You asked which works better for the four treadle pedals. They share almost everything: the same treadle, the same Classic and Chords algorithms, the same calibration, and three of the four have nearly the same lists. What differs is the list of settings, a few extra controls (the DT's drop side, the MonoNeon's Octaver and Hypersonic) and small differences in voicing.

How the combined engine runs. Every voice has one target pitch made from up to three parts added in semitones: the shift layer (the DT's drop tune or The Drop), the jump (HammerOn, Ricochet, a sequence step) and the treadle bend. One shifter produces it. Harmony, Octaver, Detune, Dry+ and octave-plus-dry add a second voice next to the dry. So a drop of −2 under a whammy bend to +12 is one shift of +10, one latency, one set of artefacts, which is also how the DT behaves (its drop and whammy run together and HammerOn's Pitch 1 is relative to the current sequence step).

Treadle section

The selector is a Karlsson dial whose positions change with the Model card. Toe up is the original pitch (or the first interval for Harmony and Octaver). Toe down is the setting's target. Whammy and Hypersonic settings are wet only. Harmony and Octaver add the dry. Detune fades a detuned copy in with the treadle.

Footswitch section

The three compact pedals become one section. The Drop always sits on footswitch D. Footswitch B plays either HammerOn's Pitch 1 or the Ricochet, chosen by a JUMP card (HAMMER or GLIDE). Footswitch C is always HammerOn's Pitch 2, as on the original. The shift layer is shared with the treadle section, so a latched drop carries across when you switch sections.

The Ricochet has one footswitch, so it stays that way (Kevin's call): in GLIDE, footswitch B is the Ricochet with one interval, direction, Shift and Return, exactly as on the pedal. C keeps HammerOn's Pitch 2, so you can glide on B and hammer on C.

The character to keep

  • Classic mode warble. Players describe Classic as the mode for the glitchy sounds associated with the original, and Chords as the clean, precise one. The warble on held, ringing strings is part of famous parts (one long-time owner points to the octave intro of "My Iron Lung"). Divebomb keeps it: the Classic tracker briefly mis-locks on chords and ringing strings like the original. A small GLITCH trim on the Deep page goes from 0 (clean) through 1 (original, default) to 2 (more).
  • Classic on chords flutters. MusicRadar heard digital flutter in Classic with chords on both Whammy and Harmony, which Chords removes. Kept, by the same tracker.
  • Dive bombs into rumble. Three octaves down sends low notes subsonic. The originals do this, so Divebomb does too, with a 20 Hz high-pass so it never reaches your speakers as pure DC.
  • Compact pedals sound a touch darker in Chords. Ricochet and HammerOn run Chords at 22 kHz. The Original bandwidth option covers it.
  • Stutter drops. Tapping a momentary Drop or DT momentary for accents.
  • Iconic sounds as factory presets: octave-up solo (Classic, 1 oct up), octave-up warble intro, octave-down "bass" riff on guitar, 12-string (octave plus dry up), whole-step stabs, dive bomb, Hypersonic squeal, MonoNeon-style octave morph on bass, techno sequencer (Sequence 5 with Dry+ and Trill).

What people complain about, and Divebomb fixes rather than copies: The Drop and older Whammys losing track of bass notes as they fade, warbly or muddy low notes on bass, and noticeable lag for fast bass grooves.

Bass, in every model

  • Tracking down to low B (30.9 Hz). The Classic tracker looks for pitches down to 28 Hz in every model, not just Bass. It runs on a 4x decimated copy so the long window low B needs is cheap, and it locks from the last period rather than waiting for the next, so the audio isn't held back while it locks. Until it locks on a new low note (one to two cycles, about 35 to 65 ms on low B), the shifted voice uses a short grain shifter, which sounds like the original's attack glitch.
  • Holding a fading note. Silence is judged against how loud you've been playing (the Speckle lesson), so a dying low B keeps its pitch instead of falling into warble.
  • Low Anchor, your blend option. A Linkwitz-Riley crossover keeps the dry signal below a set frequency (default 100 Hz, range 40 to 250 Hz) and puts the shifted signal above it. The dry low band has zero latency and sums flat with the wet. A LOW ANCHOR button sits on both sections with its frequency on a small knob. In Whammy settings (wet only) it keeps the fundamental under the bend, in Harmony and Octaver it stops two low notes fighting, and on a drop it keeps the punch while the upper harmonics shift. On by default when Input is Bass and in the NEON model (Kevin's call), off otherwise, and you can always switch it.
  • Single notes and octaves. You don't tune below low B or play chords on bass through it, so Live Classic covers your bass playing. The longer Chords windows are tuned for guitar chords.
  • Bass voicing. In the Bass model the Chords shifter uses longer low-band analysis (the Afterglow lesson: twice the window for bass) and a gentler formant handling, and Detune Shallow and Deep are scaled for bass. The Bass model's harmony list leans upward (oct up / 10th up and the like) because harmonies below a bass note turn to mud.
  • Test cases: plucked low B and E, fading notes, slap, a held fifth, and a fast sixteenth-note line at 120 BPM. The render test fails if any model is more than 5 cents out on a held low B or loses lock on a note that's still within 23 dB of your playing level.

Latency

Latency is the time a pitch-shifted note arrives after you play it. In a plugin it has two parts: what the shifter adds (fixed, reported to Ableton) and Ableton's own buffer, which you set. Shifting a note needs at least part of a cycle of it, and a low B cycle is 32 ms long, so the clean polyphonic method costs a lot on bass. Divebomb gives you a choice.

These are design targets at 48 kHz. The build will measure the real figures with a click and a plucked low B and print them on the release with CPU per instance at 64 and 128 samples, as the house rules ask. With Low Anchor on, the bass fundamental always arrives at zero added latency, which is why it matters most in Live mode. Live trades some smoothness on chords and on big downward shifts for speed. It's the default because you play bass live. Studio is for recording and resampling, where Ableton compensates the delay. A tip for the setup note: Ableton's "Reduced Latency When Monitoring" keeps other plugins' delay off your monitored track.

Treadle feel on the expression pedal

  • Calibration from the shared PedalControl: one-step heel and toe, which also fixes your pedal reading backwards on the footswitch. Heel is always toe up, the original pitch.
  • No steps. MIDI CC has 128 values, so across a two-octave bend each step is about 0.19 semitones, which you'd hear as a staircase. Divebomb measures how often values arrive and glides between them over that gap (usually 5 to 15 ms), sample by sample. It never waits for the next value, so there's no lag. 14-bit expression is read if your controller sends it.
  • Smoothing uses the house setting, default Low, so fast rocks stay fast.
  • Curve: the originals map the treadle evenly in pitch, so the default curve is Linear in semitones. Exponential and S-curve are on the Pedal page.
  • Exact ends. The last 2% at each end snaps to the exact interval, so toe down is a true octave and heel down is truly dry, even if your pedal doesn't quite reach 0 or 127.
  • In the footswitch section, the same pedal sets the Trill rate by default, or Ricochet's shift and return times. Assignable to any control per preset.

Footswitch defaults

Momentary CC 20 to 23 on channel 16 (any channel when read straight from the footswitch's USB cable), expression CC 11. Family timings: hold 0.45 s, combo window 0.12 s, combo hold 1 s. Learn remaps anything.

Why:

  • A is bypass in both sections, as in every Flip Fella plugin, and A+B switches section in both, so the switch you use to escape is always under the same foot. Your ideas for B, C and D are kept as you described them.
  • Pitch switches act on the press. A jump that waits to see if it's a hold is useless. So B, C (in the footswitch section) and D have no separate hold jobs, the hold is the job. Where a switch has a hold job (A, C in the treadle section), the tap still fires on the press and the hold adds to it after 0.45 s.
  • Combos undo the first press (the Speckle and Afterglow pattern). If B joins A within 0.12 s, A's bypass toggle is taken back before you hear it, and the section switches. If C and D are pressed together, both pitch jumps are taken back at once so Keeper's one-second hold is silent.
  • Model on B+C held 1 s (you wanted it on a footswitch). It's the one combo free in both sections. B and C's jumps are taken back as soon as the combo is seen, so nothing sounds while you hold, then the model steps and its card flips. A+B couldn't take it, because its tap already switches section on the press.
  • Keeper never clashes. C+D's tap and hold are separate events in the shared code, and no single switch's job needs C and D together.
  • D in the treadle section, tap to latch and hold for momentary, gives you both of the DT's drop footswitches on one switch. In the footswitch section D stays purely momentary by default because The Drop's quick stutter taps would otherwise latch.
  • Every footswitch job has an on-screen button.

MonoNeon skin

  • Choosing the NEON model repaints the whole panel neon yellow with neon orange, with gaffer tape strips across the panel like the tape in the pedal's customising kit (neon yellow and neon orange tape plus a marker). Tape strips carry a few hand-marked labels, as if written with the kit's marker.
  • The change runs as a flip-card wipe: the panel splits into tiles that flip left to right over 0.6 s, each turning over to its new colour, with the tape slapped on last. Any other model flips back to the dark family look the same way. Reduced motion switches instantly.
  • Pebble turns neon orange with a strip of tape across its head.
  • Readability. Every label on yellow is near-black (about 17:1 contrast). Orange is only ever a fill behind black text, never text on yellow, because orange on yellow fails. Flip cards stay black with white digits in both skins. The mockup has a contrast check built in.

Pebble

  • Stretches tall as the pitch rises and squashes flat as it dives, following the actual output pitch, not just the treadle.
  • Wobbles side to side on Detune, at the detune's beat rate.
  • Jumps on every HammerOn hit, and bounces along with Trill and sequence steps.
  • Slides down a little ladder with The Drop, and leans into Ricochet glides.
  • Blinks, reacts to input level and clipping, nods and shows KEPT for Keeper, as in every plugin.

Family conventions

  • DSP core in plain C++17 with no JUCE and no Windows-only code, with an offline render test, building for Windows x64 and Linux ARM64.
  • GitHub Actions builds the VST3 and the Linux ARM64 version in kevinwkaye/divebomb, with CPU and latency figures on each release.
  • One parameter list holds every control, footswitch action and Keeper action, for a remote controller.

Decisions (Kevin, 6 November 2026)

  1. Combined engine: yes, one treadle engine with a Model selector.
  2. Low Anchor: on by default when Input is Bass and in the NEON model, off otherwise.
  3. Range and playing: nothing below low B, no chords on bass through it. Live Classic is the bass path.
  4. DT momentary: instant by default, optional glide.
  5. Ricochet: kept as the original, one footswitch (B). C stays HammerOn's Pitch 2.
  6. B+C tap: no footswitch tap tempo. Trill syncs to Ableton by default with an on-screen Tap.
  7. Model from your feet: yes, B+C held 1 s steps the model.
  8. Glitch: as true to the original as possible. GLITCH 1 is the default in every model, bass included.
